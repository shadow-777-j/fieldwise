# PART 1 — PRODUCT REQUIREMENTS DOCUMENT

## 1. Project Overview

FIELDWISE AI is a geospatial agricultural intelligence platform that combines NASA Earth observation datasets, local agricultural information, soil characteristics and farmer preferences to identify agricultural vulnerabilities and evaluate crop-rotation strategies.

The application should help farmers and agricultural experts understand how environmental conditions have changed over time and explore alternative farming strategies.

The platform must provide:

- Historical environmental analysis.
- Satellite-based vegetation monitoring.
- Rainfall and temperature trend analysis.
- Water availability indicators.
- Soil and crop suitability assessment.
- Crop-rotation scenario comparison.
- Climate what-if simulation.
- Explainable recommendations.
- Evidence-backed agricultural reports.

### Primary objective

Convert NASA Earth observation data into practical, explainable agricultural decisions.

## 2. Target Users

1. Farmers – understand farm conditions and explore rotation options.
2. Agricultural experts – evaluate soil and crop suitability.
3. Researchers – analyse environmental trends.
4. Agricultural institutions – investigate climate-resilient farming strategies.
5. Students and developers – explore satellite-based agricultural intelligence.

## 3. Initial Demonstration Region

Start with Pokkali agricultural fields in Kumbalangi, Ernakulam, Kerala.

This farming system is particularly suitable because rice cultivation generally occurs from June to mid-October, followed by prawn farming during the higher-salinity period from November to April.

Research reference:

https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/JoSSWQ/article/view/130017

The application must support other agricultural regions and conventional crop rotations through configurable crop and soil profiles.

Do not make Pokkali-specific assumptions universal.

## 5. Core Analytical Indicators

### Climate indicators

- Seasonal rainfall totals.
- Rainfall anomalies.
- Average temperature.
- Maximum temperature.
- Temperature anomalies.
- Extreme rainfall frequency.
- Seasonal rainfall distribution.

### Vegetation indicators

NDVI:

NDVI = (NIR - Red) / (NIR + Red)

EVI:

EVI = 2.5 × (NIR - Red) / (NIR + 6 × Red - 7.5 × Blue + 1)

The application should calculate vegetation indices from appropriately scaled, cloud-masked surface reflectance.

### Water indicators

- Soil moisture.
- Soil moisture anomaly.
- Rainfall minus evapotranspiration.
- Seasonal water availability.
- Water deficit and surplus indicators.

A simplified rainfall-minus-ET calculation must be labelled as a partial water-balance indicator, not a complete hydrological model.

### Soil indicators

Accept farmer-entered or laboratory-derived:

- Soil pH.
- Electrical conductivity.
- Organic carbon.
- Nitrogen.
- Phosphorus.
- Potassium.
- Soil texture.
- Soil moisture.
- Salinity.

NASA observations must not be represented as direct measurements of soil pH, EC or nutrient concentrations.

### Module D: Farm Vulnerability

Generate five indicators:

1. Water stress.
2. Heat exposure.
3. Excess rainfall/flood exposure.
4. Vegetation deterioration.
5. Soil degradation risk.

Show the methodology and confidence of each indicator.

Do not invent a risk percentage. Where there is insufficient evidence, display insufficient data.

### Module E: Rotation Lab

This is the most important module.

Compare at least three scenarios:

A. Existing farming system.

B. Soil improvement strategy.

C. Climate-adaptive strategy.

For conventional farms, support locally appropriate rice–pulse, cereal–legume and cover-crop rotation options where agronomically feasible.

For Pokkali, preserve the traditional rice–prawn baseline and evaluate realistic modifications without automatically treating aquaculture as a crop to replace.

Compare:

- Water requirements.
- Soil improvement potential.
- Nitrogen fixation.
- Organic matter contribution.
- Growing-season compatibility.
- Salinity tolerance.
- Waterlogging tolerance.
- Pest-cycle interruption.
- Erosion protection.
- Market suitability.
- Farmer preferences.

### Module F: Recommendation Engine

Use a hybrid decision model.

First, apply hard agronomic constraints.

Exclude unsuitable crops based on:
- Soil salinity.
- pH.
- Waterlogging.
- Growing-season duration.
- Temperature limits.
- Water availability.
- Disease constraints.
- Farmer limitations.

Then use multi-criteria scoring.

Initial weights:

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Water sustainability | 25% |
| Soil improvement potential | 25% |
| Climate resilience | 20% |
| Economic suitability | 15% |
| Erosion protection | 10% |
| Farmer preference | 5% |

Formula:

Final Score = Σ (Criterion Score × Criterion Weight)

Normalise scores to 0–100.

Allow farmers to modify priorities, but never allow a preference score to override a hard agronomic exclusion.

The recommendation must explain the factors behind its ranking.

### Module G: Climate Simulator

Allow hypothetical adjustments:

- Rainfall: -20% to +20%.
- Temperature: -1°C to +3°C.
- Water availability: -30% to +30%.
- Growing-season shift: -30 to +30 days.

Recalculate scenario suitability.

Clearly identify these as user-defined hypothetical scenarios, not official NASA forecasts.

### Module H: Decision Report

Generate a PDF report containing:

- Farm profile.
- Analysis period.
- Data sources.
- Environmental indicators.
- Historical trends.
- Vulnerability assessment.
- Three rotation strategies.
- Comparative scores.
- Supporting evidence.
- Limitations.
- Recommendations for local validation.

## 8. Data Resolution and Reliability

Never resample coarse satellite data to a finer grid and imply that it has gained additional spatial detail.

Display the native resolution of every source.

Use:
- Landsat for field-scale vegetation analysis.
- MODIS for broader and longer-term vegetation analysis.
- GPM for regional precipitation patterns.
- SMAP for regional soil moisture.
- NASA POWER for regional meteorological context.
- Local soil measurements for field-level soil chemistry.

For small farms, explain that coarse products may not distinguish conditions between neighbouring fields.

The application should look like a professional geospatial intelligence SaaS platform.

Design direction:
- Premium scientific dashboard.
- Natural agricultural green combined with deep navy.
- Light neutral backgrounds.
- High-contrast data visualization.
- Clear typography.
- Professional map controls.
- Minimal visual clutter.
- Responsive layouts.
- Consistent spacing and alignment.

Avoid:
- Generic AI chatbot appearance.
- Excessive gradients.
- Unnecessary glassmorphism.
- Oversized cards.
- Excessive rounded containers.
- Decorative statistics without real data.
- Fake maps and fake satellite measurements.

## 10. Definition of Done

The MVP is complete when:

1. A user can create or select a farm.
2. The system can retrieve actual NASA data.
3. Historical analysis can be generated.
4. At least three rotation scenarios can be compared.
5. Each scenario has explainable scores.
6. Missing information is clearly identified.
7. The application can generate a report.
8. The entire application works responsively.
9. No fabricated measurements are presented as real observations.
10. The application can demonstrate its functionality from beginning to end.

### 5. SCIENTIFIC PROCESSING

Implement:

NDVI = (NIR - Red) / (NIR + Red)

EVI = 2.5 × (NIR - Red) / (NIR + 6 × Red - 7.5 × Blue + 1)

Rainfall anomaly = Observed seasonal rainfall - Historical seasonal mean.

Standardised soil moisture anomaly = (Current soil moisture - Seasonal mean) / Seasonal standard deviation.

Use matching seasonal baselines.

For all indicators:
- Document the calculation.
- Track missing observations.
- Display data quality.
- Preserve native spatial resolution.
- Avoid misleading spatial interpolation.
